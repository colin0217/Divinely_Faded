02 / THE SHAPE

Why it's called a burst.

It radiates out from the ear.

Most fades run in a level band around the head. A burst fade curves instead, fanning out from behind the ear and leaving the nape and the back with more length. That is why it pairs so naturally with curly tops, faux hawks and mullets: the shape keeps weight exactly where those styles need it.

03 / WHO IT SUITS

Curls show it off.

Straight hair can wear it too.

Curly and wavy hair shows the burst most clearly, because the volume on top and in back holds its form against the bare arc. On straight hair the result looks sleeker and a little more subtle. If you need a quieter outline for work, a low taper is the calmer cousin.

05 / UPKEEP

Keep the arc crisp.

The curve goes soft first.

The half-circle around the ear is the detail that loses definition first, since it is cut the shortest. The length in back is more forgiving as it grows. If the arc is the part you care about, text me and ask how soon to rebook for it.

06 / QUESTIONS

Burst fade basics.

What does burst fade mean?+

A fade shaped like a semicircle around the ear. It is shortest right by the ear and gets longer as it moves outward, so it seems to burst out from that point.

Is a burst fade just a mullet?+

No, though they are often worn together. The burst is the fade around the ear; a mullet is about the length in back. Put them together and you get the burst fade mullet, which is covered on the modern mullet page.

Burst fade vs. taper: what is the difference?+

A taper shortens the sideburns and nape in fairly straight lines. A burst curves around the ear and leaves the back fuller. Both are subtle compared with a full skin fade.

Does a burst fade work on straight hair?+

It does. The arc is a little less dramatic than on curls, and the top usually needs some texture cut into it so it doesn't lie completely flat.
