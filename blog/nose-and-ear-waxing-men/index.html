Nose and Ear Waxing for Men: What to Expect

Nose and ear waxing at my shop is an add-on, $10 on top of the service you book, and it already comes with the Luxury Grooming Experience. Wax goes on, sets for a moment and comes off with the stray hair, so the nostrils and ears look clean rather than clipped.

Here's what to expect in the chair, how waxing compares with trimming, and how to add it to your next appointment.

By Tim Murillo

01 / IN THE CHAIR

How it goes.

Four quick steps.

The wax

Warm wax goes onto a small applicator and into the opening of the nostril, or onto the outer ear where hair grows. Only the hair you can see at the edge is the target.

The set

The wax sits for a moment and firms up around the hair. Nothing to do but hold still.

The pull

One quick pull and it's out, hair and all. Expect a sharp sting and watery eyes for a second, and then it's over.

The check

I look over both sides, catch any strays left behind, and we carry on with the rest of your appointment.

02 / WAX OR TRIM

Why wax instead of trim.

Root versus surface.

A trimmer cuts hair at the surface, so the stubble is still there and shows again soon. Wax lifts the hair out from the root, which means the area stays clean longer before anything grows back in.

Ear hair makes the case for wax on its own. It grows on the outer ear and along the rim, where a trimmer can skip fine hairs, and the wax catches all of it in one pass.

If you'd rather keep trimming at home, that's your call. The add-on is there for when you'd rather hand it off.

03 / WHO BOOKS IT

The detail others spot first.

You rarely see it. Everyone else does.

Nose and ear hair is easy to miss in your own mirror, because you look at yourself straight on. Other people see you from the side, across a table or in a photo, where a stray hair at the ear or the nostril stands out.

That's why the add-on pairs so naturally with a fresh cut. A sharp fade and a clean line-up draw attention to the face and the ears, so tidying both finishes what the cut started.

It makes sense before anything with a camera or a handshake in it: an interview, a wedding, a family photo. It also makes sense on a regular visit, simply because you're already in the chair.
