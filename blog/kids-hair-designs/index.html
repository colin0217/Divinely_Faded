Hair Designs for Kids: What Parents Should Know

A hair design is a line or shape cut into the short part of a fade. For kids, it goes on top of a regular haircut as a $10 add-on on Booksy, and it turns a plain fade into one that is theirs.

Before you book one, here is what to know: the two kinds of design, how to add one on Booksy, why it fades, and whether school will mind.

By Tim Murillo

01 / THE OPTIONS

Lines or freestyle.

Two kinds of design.

Lines are straight cuts set side by side, like the set above the ear in the photo at the top of this post. Freestyle follows the curve of the head, the way a sweep might run from the temple around to the back of the ear.

Both sit where the fade is shortest, so the shape reads clean against the skin. That is also why a design shows up sharpest on a short fade, where there is enough contrast for the lines to stand out.

If your kid has a picture of a design they like, bring it. If not, ask them where they want it and how bold, and we will settle the details in the chair.

03 / HOW LONG IT LASTS

Designs grow out.

Plan the date around that.

A design is cut into hair that is already very short, so it is the first part of the cut to soften as the hair comes back. How quickly depends on how fast your kid's hair grows.

That makes timing matter. If the design is for a birthday, a big game or picture day, book the cut close to that date rather than weeks ahead.

Want it back once it fades? Book the haircut again and add the design again. Each visit is its own appointment.

04 / SCHOOL

Check the rules first.

Every school sets its own.

Some schools and sports teams have rules about designs cut into hair. I don't know your school's dress code, so look it up before you book, especially for a bold freestyle piece.

Picture day is the other date parents plan around. A fresh design photographs crisp, so the week of the photo is a good time to book it.

05 / PLACEMENT

Where it goes.

The side of the head, mostly.

Above or behind the ear

Designs tend to go on the side of the head, above or behind the ear, because that is where a fade runs shortest. Both kids' designs on my hair designs page sit there, one sweeping back past the ear and one set just over it.

Simple or bold is the other choice. A few straight lines is the quieter option for a kid who wants something small. A freestyle curve stands out more. Let your kid pick, then check it against the school rules.

Getting your kid ready

Show your kid a photo of the design before the visit so they know what they are getting and can change their mind at home, not in the chair.

Remind them that straight lines depend on a still head. Sitting still while the lines go in is the part that makes them look clean.
